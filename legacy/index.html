TicketRush: ticketing that holds up when everyone buys at once

A ticket drop is the worst five minutes for any checkout: thousands arrive together, two people click the same seat, and the payment provider times out mid-charge. I designed and built TicketRush around those failures.

My role: product scope, system design, UI, and the full stack: Next.js, Postgres, Auth.js, payments, tests and CI.

Next.jsTypeScriptPostgresDrizzleAuth.jsStripe test modeVitestGitHub Actions
Try the live app ↗ Code ↗ System design ↗
0seats held twice when 3,000 buyers raced for 500 seats
2,000simultaneous joins to the waiting room, 2,000 unique places
12 → 2database round trips to hold seats, after collapsing the path into one atomic SQL statement
783 → 310 msmedian hold → pay → tickets flow with a simulated 10 ms database round trip (local measurement)

Failure

Two buyers, one seat

All-or-nothing holds, plus a unique (event, seat) rule as the final word.

Failure

Gateway times out

Idempotency keys mean retries return the same payment. No double charges.

Failure

Webhook never arrives

A reconciler runs every 5 minutes and issues tickets or refunds.

Failure

Someone skips the line

The waiting room is enforced on the server, not in the page.

How I work

The same loop whether the title says PM, designer or engineer.

Step 1

Find the friction

Tickets, usage data and watching people use the product show where it actually breaks.

Step 2

Design the system

The flow, the data model, the API contract and the failure cases, agreed before code.

Step 3

Build it to hold

Accessible UI, secure by default, and tested against the load and failures it will meet.

Step 4

Measure

Tickets, load times and task completion after release feed the next round.
